Potential Landscape

Type in a grid of voltage readings — from a real conductive-paper probe, a multimeter, or a simulation's voltage sensor — and get an instant 3D "landscape" of the electric potential plus a crisp equipotential/field map. This replaces the old by-hand Excel bar chart, without the pixelation.

1Collect your data (10–15 min)

  1. Open PhET — Charges and Fields and turn on Grid and Values.
  2. Build your electrode shape from point charges: a row of them for a plate, a ring of them for a circular electrode.
  3. Drag the voltage sensor to each grid point in the table below and read off the exact voltage (no pixelation — it's an exact number).
  4. Type each reading into the matching cell in the table. Leave a cell blank to skip a point.

Prefer the physical version instead? Real conductive-paper + multimeter readings drop straight into the same table.

Analysis questions
  1. Why can each electrode be treated as a single equipotential, even though it isn't a single point?
  2. Where are the field arrows longest? What does that tell you about how closely spaced the equipotential lines are there?
  3. Confirm: do the field arrows cross the equipotential lines at right angles everywhere on your map?
  4. For the parallel-plate data, how does the voltage change moving from one plate to the other? What does that imply about the field between the plates?
  5. For the concentric-ring data, where is the field strongest? Does that match |E| ∝ 1/r?

Built for a 90-minute block: ~15 min sampling in a simulation or on conductive paper, ~10 min typing values in here, the rest for the field-map questions and discussion. The grid, sliders, and toggles are all live — changing a value updates both views immediately.
